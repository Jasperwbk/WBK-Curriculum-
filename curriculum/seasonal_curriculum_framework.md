# Seasonal Curriculum Framework — Project Two

## Overview
This framework organizes the kids' curriculum around the four seasons rather than
fixed grade levels, in a land-based, Nordic-style approach where lessons follow what
is actually happening outdoors. Each season is anchored to a solstice/equinox
touchpoint (echoing the Asatru wheel of the year) and to real planting/harvest
timing for our garden zone (Seymour, Missouri — USDA zone 6b, on the edge of 7a).

Each kid gets a simple generic age-equivalent grade label so they have a normal
answer if other kids ask "what grade are you in," but internally the actual
curriculum is tailored per kid based on their individual assessment starting point,
not the label.

The same seasonal/calendar structure links to Sarah's garden planning, so garden
work, canning projects, and kid lessons can be scheduled together as one system
rather than two separate calendars.

## Fall — Harvest & Preserving
- Calendar anchor: autumn equinox into Winter Nights (~late October), marking the
  end of harvest and start of the colder season.
- Garden tie-in: final harvests, seed saving, canning and preserving what's been
  grown, putting the garden to bed for winter.
- Kid learning themes: where food comes from and how to make it last (canning,
  drying, root cellaring), counting/measuring through harvest and recipes, writing
  through garden/harvest journaling, history of how people historically prepared
  for winter without modern refrigeration.
- Skald's role: prompt Sarah on seed-saving and canning planning as this hits;
  suggest printable worksheets themed around harvest math and journaling.

## Winter — Survival Skills & Deep Focus
- Calendar anchor: winter solstice / Yule, the longest night, turning back toward
  the light.
- Garden tie-in: planning next year's garden and seed orders, indoor seed-starting
  prep, reviewing what worked and didn't from the past year.
- Kid learning themes: survival and off-grid skills (fire-building, layering,
  water safety in cold), heavier book-work and reading time since outdoor hours
  are more limited, mechanical/hands-on indoor projects, storytelling and lore by
  the fire.
- Skald's role: this is a good season to push a bit more of the printable/book-work
  side, and to help Sarah simplify next year's garden plan into something workable.

## Spring — Planting & New Life
- Calendar anchor: spring equinox / Ostara, new growth returning.
- Garden tie-in: actual zone 6b/7a planting windows for direct-sow and transplants;
  tending new chicks/rabbit litters if applicable; watching the stream and land
  wake up.
- Kid learning themes: biology and life cycles taught hands-on through the garden
  and animals, math through spacing/measuring garden rows, science through
  observing what's returning to the land.
- Skald's role: help Sarah organize seed inventory and plot the actual planting
  calendar against zone 6b/7a windows; suggest matching printables.

## Summer — Building & Water
- Calendar anchor: summer solstice / Midsummer, the longest day.
- Garden tie-in: active growing season, watering routines, early harvests.
- Kid learning themes: most outdoor hours of the year — hands-on building projects,
  water-based learning at the stream (biology, physics of flow, fishing as a
  teaching tool), longer outdoor blocks for core subjects taught in the field.
- Skald's role: this is the season to lean hardest into outdoor-led teaching and
  keep book-work light and flexible.

## Expandability
- Each seasonal block is a container, not a fixed script — outside curriculum or
  books picked up on sale can be dropped into whichever season/subject they best
  fit.
- Wolf Blossom Kronicles character art (Kira for Millaray, Rhoe for Macayo, Nova
  for Maizley) can be incorporated into printables for each season once art files
  are uploaded — mostly black and white for easy printing.
- This framework is designed to receive the individual kid assessment results as
  its starting input, so each child's actual seasonal curriculum can be
  tailor-built from their real starting point rather than a generic grade level.

## Reference Source for Seasonal/Spiritual Content
Northwoods Kindred (an Asatru/Norse heathen practice and content group) is a
values-aligned reference source Jasper follows for the seasonal ritual and spiritual
side of this framework. Their content on seasonal gatherings, rituals, and honoring
ancestors can be drawn on for flavor and ideas when building out the Asatru-calendar
touchpoints in each season above. Their companion channel, Kindred Homestead, covers
homestead crafts and practical skills and can be drawn on for hands-on project ideas
across the seasonal blocks as well.

## In-World Teaching Characters
Since each kid's curriculum is individually tailored, no two are alike, and we can
lean into that with a fun personal touch: each kid's own animated Wolf Blossom
Kronicles counterpart can appear as a little pop-up guide on their worksheets and
printables, teaching or introducing that particular activity in character (Kira for
Millaray, Rhoe for Macayo, Nova for Maizley). Jasper's own character can appear
throughout as a narrator-style presence, tying activities back to the wider story.
This will need the character art uploaded before it can be built into the actual
printables.
